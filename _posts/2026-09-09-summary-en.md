## [OpenAI Claims Navier-Stokes Solution, Priority Dispute Erupts](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/8/on-navier-stokes/) ⭐️ 10.0/10

On September 8, 2026, OpenAI announced that its unreleased AI model, using a swarm of about 10,000 agents, produced a resolution to the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem, one of the Millennium Prize Problems. The result, which is a counter-example showing breakdown of solutions, was formalized in Lean, but has not yet been verified by external mathematicians. If verified, this would be the first AI-discovered solution to a Millennium Prize Problem, marking a paradigm shift in mathematical research and demonstrating the potential of frontier AI models. The accompanying priority dispute with mathematicians from Anthropic raises critical questions about research ethics, data access, and collaboration in the age of AI. OpenAI stated that the agents sent 4.9 million messages and used about 300 billion output tokens across all attempted problems, with the Navier-Stokes resolution alone using 130 billion tokens. The company said it would decline the $1 million prize if the solution were accepted. The method built on a 2023 approach by Diego Córdoba and Luis Martínez-Zoroa for finding blowup in related fluid equations.

rss · Simon Willison · Sep 8, 23:55

**Background**: The Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem asks whether solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations, which describe fluid motion, always remain smooth and globally defined in three dimensions. It is one of seven Millennium Prize Problems established by the Clay Mathematics Institute in 2000, each carrying a $1 million prize. As of 2026, only the Poincaré conjecture has been officially solved, with Grigori Perelman declining the award.
